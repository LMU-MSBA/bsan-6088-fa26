## Your task

Use the Meridian stakeholder interview to show how your understanding of the
business problem changed. We are evaluating your judgment, not how polished
your AI output looks. The class interview and worksheet count toward this work.
You do not need to arrange another interview. Use your before-and-after notes
and DCACT scope check to support the submission below.

Submit one clearly organized document or Markdown file, with links to your V1
where needed. Include these four items from the revised syllabus:

1. **Problem statement after the stakeholder interview.** Identify the business
   gap, decision and owner, options, consequence, deadline, and success criteria.
   State what still needs confirmation. Use interview evidence to explain your
   framing, distinguishing simulation facts, public research, and inferences.
2. **A V1 of a solution with a visual from your coding agent.** Build a small
   first version that supports the problem you identified. Include the visual
   and a direct link to the work, and explain how it helps the decision. A
   prototype or mockup is sufficient; a completed dashboard, data pull, or
   validated analysis is not required. Label illustrative data and assumptions.
   Explain what you accepted, changed, or rejected from the agent and why.
3. **Assumptions you are proceeding on.** Explain the risk if each is wrong
   and how or with whom you will verify it. Distinguish assumptions from facts
   and known unknowns.
4. **One question you wish you had asked.** Write the actual question and
   explain how its answer could change your problem statement, criteria, or
   scope. Use a question that remains unanswered after the interviews.

Your original statement, interview notes, and DCACT worksheet are working
materials, not additional deliverables. Cite the client brief, interview
simulation, and any public sources you use. If you played Dana, distinguish
role-card evidence from answers elicited in the interview.

## What meets the standard

| Criterion from the syllabus | Evidence in this deliverable |
|---|---|
| Completion | All four required items are present: post-interview problem statement, V1 with an agent-created visual, assumptions, and the missed question. The file and linked work open. |
| Effort | Specific interview evidence supports your reasoning. You examine a consequential uncertainty rather than merely rewording the brief. |
| Learning | Your revision frames a decision rather than accepting the dashboard request. You distinguish facts, proposals, and assumptions, and can explain the limits of your analysis. |

There is no single correct final statement. A reasonable alternative framing
can meet the standard when the interview evidence supports it. An invented
profitability target, an unsupported forecast, or an AI-written statement you
cannot explain does not demonstrate learning.

## AI and confidentiality

Use your agent to critique your statement or organize your notes after you
have written your initial statement. For example:

```text
Read my original statement, interview notes, and revised statement.
Identify any claim the notes do not support. Distinguish facts, assumptions,
and proposed success criteria. Ask me about gaps; do not invent Dana's answers.
Check whether the decision, owner, deadline, and analytics stopping point are clear.
Show suggested corrections and let me decide what to change.
```

Check every suggested correction against your notes. Never use AI to generate
an interview you did not conduct. The brief's boundaries apply: customer
records and employee data, including excerpts, cannot go into any AI tool or
your public repository. Store-week sales totals and store attributes are
permitted for AI use; confirm permission for any other internal material.
This assignment uses fictional conversation notes and public research, so
there is no reason to obtain or upload restricted records.

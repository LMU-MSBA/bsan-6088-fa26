Your task

Use the Meridian stakeholder interview to show how your understanding of the business problem changed. We are evaluating your judgment, not how polished your AI output looks. The class interview and worksheet count toward this work. You do not need to arrange another interview or build a dashboard.

Submit one document of roughly 2–3 pages, or the equivalent Markdown file. Tables may make it longer. Include these sections:

  1. Before the interview. Include your original problem statement, unchanged, from before the interview and before you read the role card. Aim for 75–150 words.
  2. After the interview. Include your revised statement, 100–200 words. Identify the business gap, decision and owner, options, consequence, deadline, and success criteria. Where criteria are unresolved, state who must confirm them. Separate the three-week board checkpoint from the tentative eight-week engagement.
  3. What changed and why. Explain at least three meaningful changes using specific interview answers or corrections. For each, connect the evidence to the change and its business implication. If an important part did not change, explain what confirmed it. If you played Dana, label role-card evidence separately and use the team's questions and read-back corrections to explain what you learned; do not present private card reading as an interview answer.
  4. Assumptions. Identify at least three consequential assumptions you are proceeding on, the risk if each is wrong, and how or with whom you will verify it. Distinguish these from facts and known unknowns.
  5. One question you wish you had asked. Write the actual question and explain how the answer could change your problem statement, success criteria, or scope. Use an unanswered question, not one already resolved in round 2.
  6. DCACT scope check. Briefly address all five steps using the worksheet. Explain where your analysis would stop and why the available evidence and decision justify that depth. No data pull or completed analysis is required.
  7. Your judgment and sources. Link the Workshop 1 wiki claim and original source that informed an interview question. Note one AI suggestion you accepted, changed, or rejected and explain why, or state that you did not use AI for this deliverable. Identify the client brief and interview simulation as sources; do not present fictional responses as real research findings.

What meets the standard

Criterion from the syllabus Evidence in this deliverable
Completion All required sections are present, including both statements, changes, assumptions, and the missed question. The file opens.
Effort Specific interview evidence supports your reasoning. You examine a consequential uncertainty rather than merely rewording the brief.
Learning Your revision frames a decision rather than accepting the dashboard request. You distinguish facts, proposals, and assumptions, and can explain the limits of your analysis.

There is no single correct final statement. A reasonable alternative framing can meet the standard when the interview evidence supports it. An invented profitability target, an unsupported forecast, or an AI-written statement you cannot explain does not demonstrate learning.

AI and confidentiality

Use your agent to critique your statement or organize your notes after you have written your initial statement. For example:

Read my original statement, interview notes, and revised statement.
Identify any claim the notes do not support. Distinguish facts, assumptions,
and proposed success criteria. Ask me about gaps; do not invent Dana's answers.
Check whether the decision, owner, deadline, and analytics stopping point are clear.
Show suggested corrections and let me decide what to change.

Check every suggested correction against your notes. Never use AI to generate an interview you did not conduct. The brief's boundaries apply: customer records and employee data, including excerpts, cannot go into any AI tool or your public repository. Store-week sales totals and store attributes are permitted for AI use; confirm permission for any other internal material. This assignment uses fictional conversation notes and public research, so there is no reason to obtain or upload restricted records.

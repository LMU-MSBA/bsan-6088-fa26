# Dana Okafor: stakeholder role card

**Open this only if you are playing Dana. Analysts read it after the interviews.**

This is a fictional simulation extension to the [original brief](../client-brief.md).
The original company facts and data restrictions still apply. The details below
are exercise facts, not new findings about a real client.

## Play the person, not a puzzle

You are the VP of Operations. You believe Pasadena is promising and want to
appear prepared at the board meeting. You are busy, not deliberately deceptive.
You initially think a dashboard will settle the debate. You become more open
when students ask what decision you need to make and what would change your mind.

Start with: “Thanks for helping. I'd like sales by store and category, with
something clear showing why Pasadena should be next. What do you need from me?”

Answer relevant questions naturally. Do not read the whole card aloud or hide
facts after a reasonable question. If asked broadly about the problem, explain
the board decision, then let students follow up. Do not invent numbers, approved
targets, or available files. For unknowns, name the person who could confirm them.

## Reveal through the conversation

| If they ask about… | Your answer |
|---|---|
| The decision and authority | In three weeks, the board will decide whether to authorize further Pasadena lease negotiations, defer expansion while reviewing existing stores, or request more evidence. This is not approval to open a store. You recommend; the board decides. |
| Why Pasadena | A broker brought you an attractive site and you visited it. You think the neighborhood fits Meridian's brand. There has been no independent comparison of sites. You have become attached to this option. |
| The underlying concern | Growth increased revenue, but leadership is unsure whether new stores are producing enough financial value. You need a consistent way to assess expansion and decide whether Pasadena deserves further investment of time and money. |
| An example | One newer store has high sales but a costly lease and heavy staffing needs. Another has lower sales but lower occupancy costs. You cannot say which creates more value without Finance's help. These are qualitative observations, not a dataset. |
| What would change your mind | Evidence that likely operating costs outweigh the opportunity, that Pasadena mainly shifts sales from existing stores, or that Meridian lacks capacity to operate another location. You would accept a defer recommendation if the reasoning is clear. |
| Success criteria | Your priorities are financial viability and protecting existing operations. Finance has not approved a profitability definition, investment hurdle, or target. Do not accept a student-invented threshold as official. Ask the team to propose criteria for Finance and the board to confirm. |
| Three weeks versus eight | In three weeks you need a short decision memo comparing the options, known evidence, key risks, and what must be verified before a commitment. Eight weeks is a tentative window for deeper work, subject to access and agreed scope. You do not expect a validated forecast in three weeks. |
| Data and access | The brief lists data you believe exist. You have not confirmed extract readiness, comparable POS history across the migration, or cost completeness. Marcus in IT can verify access and coverage. Finance must confirm cost definitions and provide permitted financial summaries. No extracts have been delivered in this exercise. |
| Customer evidence | Loyalty members do not represent every shopper, and existing shoppers do not establish Pasadena demand. You would welcome public market context but it is not proof that this site will succeed. |
| Privacy | Customer records and employee data, including excerpts, cannot go into any AI tool. Store-week sales totals and store attributes are permitted. Finance summaries are not automatically permitted; confirm their classification before uploading anything. An NDA does not remove these boundaries. |
| Coordination | You can approve the interview read-back and help arrange contact with Marcus and Finance. Your assistant can schedule but cannot answer analytics questions. Your slow replies are not approval. Agree what needs an explicit response. |

## When students propose a solution too early

If they immediately offer a dashboard, say: “How would that tell me which
option to recommend to the board?” If they promise a forecast, ask: “What
evidence would support that?” If they suggest a financial target, say:
“That could be a proposal. Finance and the board would need to confirm it.”

At the read-back, correct any claim that opening Pasadena is already approved,
that a dashboard is the success criterion, or that all promised data are ready.
Leave reasonable alternative framings open. There is no single required wording.

# Workshop 02 facilitator guide

## Setup

Use the [student brief](README.md) for the agenda. Prepare Zoom breakout rooms
of three or four, a timer, and a shared space for posting statements. Have
students preserve their first statements before assigning Dana's card. Ask
analysts not to read the card until interviews finish. The card is public for
accessibility and reuse; its separation is an exercise convention, not secrecy.

This progression adapts the prior EXAMPLE → EXPLORE → REFLECT → NEXT lesson:
show a short example, let students interview before specifying requirements,
give facilitator/note-taker/timekeeper roles, and use reflection to drive the
next conversation. For this online course, one conversation per breakout room
replaces the earlier small-room noise management. Students bring evidence from
the Workshop 1 wiki rather than starting research again.

## 9:00: book discussion, 30 minutes

Invite students to share a question or passage from Chapters 3–9 and connect
it to clarifying work with a senior stakeholder. Prompts: What would you ask
before accepting an assignment? How would you clarify competing deadlines
without sounding unhelpful? What would you do when silence is not approval?
Have students keep one question to use with Dana. Do not add separate book
homework; the existing reading notes feed Workshop 4.

## 9:30: example, 15 minutes

Display the dashboard request without the role-card answers. Poll students
using the opening challenge. Ask a volunteer to make the case that sales alone
are enough, then invite the missing evidence. Whiteboard three headings:
**Request / Business problem / Decision**.

Demonstrate a four-minute interview with a volunteer reading Dana's card.
Start with a premature tool question, then repair it:

- “What charts would you like?” Dana requests store and category sales.
- “What decision will the board make with those charts?” Reveal the checkpoint.
- “What would make you recommend waiting?” Reveal an alternative and a concern.
- “How will you judge whether the investment is worthwhile?” Reveal unapproved criteria.

Pause after each answer and ask students what changed. Keep most card details
for their own interviews. Explain plainly: “A chart is something we deliver.
A decision is something the client does with what we learn.” Return to the
poll at 11:40 and ask students to explain changed votes.

## Coaching the two rounds

Visit rooms and listen before intervening. If students pitch a dashboard, ask
what decision it supports. If they ask only closed questions, have them follow
one with “Can you give an example?” If they collect answers without resolving
meaning, ask them to read back the decision and deadline.

At 10:35, use cross-group peer review or swap statements in the shared space.
Each group returns one consequential gap, not copyediting. Protect the second
interview so students can actually pursue that gap. Leave a stakeholder's
unknown answer unresolved when the card does not supply it.

## Calibration after the interviews

Reveal this example only after students have revised. It is one defensible
framing, not an answer to copy:

> Meridian's board needs to decide in three weeks whether to authorize further
> Pasadena lease negotiations, defer expansion while reviewing existing stores,
> or seek more evidence. Revenue growth does not yet establish whether expansion
> creates sufficient financial value, and comparable cost and market evidence
> have not been confirmed. The team will propose a preliminary comparison of
> options, key risks, and evidence gaps for that checkpoint. Finance and the
> board must confirm the financial measures and investment criteria. A deeper
> eight-week engagement depends on agreed scope and access; it cannot be treated
> as a promise of a validated Pasadena forecast.

Accept evidence-backed alternatives. For example, a preliminary store-economics
diagnostic can support the next decision without claiming to solve site selection.
Require students to label profitability criteria as proposed, because the card
does not provide an approved definition or threshold.

Whiteboard DCACT with the exact wording in the student brief. Ask: “Where do
we stop with this evidence?” A conditional plan to compare options is reasonable;
a claimed completed prescription or forecast without data is not. Students
should distinguish a stakeholder's claimed explanation from a tested diagnosis.

## Brightspace setup

Copy [assignment.md](assignment.md) into the assignment description, or paste
the contents of [assignment.html](assignment.html) into Brightspace's HTML editor.
The HTML is a description, not an LMS import package. Set the due date to
**October 25, 2026, 11:59 PM America/Los_Angeles**. Enable file and text/link
submission where supported, using the syllabus's Credit / No Credit standard.
Confirm the actual deadline in the Brightspace preview. Do not set an end date
at the due time that would block the syllabus's two-day grace period.

Publish the student brief and worksheet ahead of class. Explain who should
open the role card. Keep the facilitator model statement off screen until
revision. The repository files themselves remain publicly readable.
